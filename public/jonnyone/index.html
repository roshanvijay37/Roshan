How it was cut

One continuous 51-second take became ten shots. Same room, same game, same framing throughout — which means the order of the source footage carries no information. Shots were picked on merit and reordered freely; the hero moment sits at 0:00 rather than where it happened at 0:27.

The hook is eye contact. Three seconds in the middle of the take are the only ones where Jonny looks straight down the lens in a play-bow. On a feed where the first second decides everything, that beat is worth more at the front than in sequence.

Every shot has its own punch-in, between 1.00× and 1.18×. Consecutive cuts from one continuous take otherwise read as glitches; varying the framing makes the same cuts read as deliberate.

Captions sit in the top third. The bottom fifth of a Short is covered by the title, channel and description, and the right edge by the button rail. The top third is also the only part of this frame that is empty floor, so nothing covers the dog.

No voiceover, on purpose. The source audio is family chatter over the dog — warm, but not scripted, and Whisper could not transcribe it above 47% confidence. A synthetic voice over pet footage reads as low-effort and tends to suppress reach. The growling and the cloth are the sound; the captions carry the narrative.

Graded and cleaned. Light denoise, contrast and saturation lift, mild sharpening. Audio high-passed at 70 Hz, gently compressed and normalised to −14.3 LUFS at −0.9 dBFS peak — the level YouTube normalises to, so nothing is turned down on the way in.
