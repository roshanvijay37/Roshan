Privacy Policy

StatusHD — com.statushd

Last updated: 18 September 2026

There are two builds of StatusHD, and they differ on exactly this point. The version on Google Play never sends your video anywhere. A separate build, shared directly with a few people, does — that is its entire purpose. Check which one you have before reading further: the version is at the bottom of the app's settings screen.

The Google Play version (1.7.x) — nothing leaves your phone

This version collects no data at all. It has no server, no account and no analytics. Your videos are read, processed and written entirely on your own device and are never transmitted anywhere.

That is verifiable rather than a promise: it does not request the Android INTERNET permission. Without it, Android itself prevents the app from opening any network connection — so it could not upload your videos, or anything else, even if it were asked to. You can confirm this yourself; the permission list on the Google Play listing shows no network access.

The shared build (1.9.x, “drop”) — your video is sent to someone's computer

This build does request INTERNET, and when you tap Send to laptop it uploads the video you picked to a personal computer operated by Roshan Vijay in Mangalore, India. If that is not what you want, use the Google Play version instead.

Being specific about what that means, because “uploads to a laptop” is easy to wave past:

  • The video is sent over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) to drop.roshanvijay.com, which points at that computer.
  • It is saved on that computer as an ordinary file, converted to 1080p, and then sent into Roshan's own WhatsApp account so he can forward it on.
  • Roshan can watch it, as can anyone with access to that computer. It is not encrypted at rest and there is no mechanism preventing him from seeing it.
  • Uploaded files are not deleted automatically. They stay on that computer until someone removes them by hand.
  • The endpoint is deliberately open: no account, no password. Anyone who knows the address can send a video to it. Nothing identifies who sent what, beyond the filename you sent and the ordinary network logs any web server keeps.
  • Nothing is sold, published, or given to any third party or analytics service. It goes to that one computer and into that one WhatsApp account.

The practical advice: only send videos you are content for Roshan to have a copy of. For anything private, use the Google Play version, which cannot send anything anywhere.

What the app does with your videos

  • Reads the video you pick, using your phone's own media APIs.
  • Analyses it and re-encodes it with your phone's hardware video encoder.
  • Writes the result to your device, in Movies/StatusHD, only when you tap Save.
  • Hands the file to another app — WhatsApp, Instagram or anything else — only when you tap Share, using Android's standard share sheet. What that app then does with it is governed by its own privacy policy, not this one.

What is stored on your device

  • Optimised videos you chose to save, in your normal gallery storage.
  • A small list of those videos (file name, date, size, resolution) kept in the app's private storage so the "My Optimized Videos" screen can show them. Deleting the app removes it.
  • Temporary working files in the app's cache during processing, cleared automatically.

None of this list leaves the device. The video itself leaves only in the shared build, and only when you tap Send to laptop, as described above. None of it is readable by other apps.

    Children

    StatusHD is a general-purpose video utility. The Google Play version collects no personal information from anyone, including children. The shared build receives whatever video the sender chose to send, which is why it is given only to people who have been told plainly where their video goes.

    Third parties

    There are no advertising SDKs, no analytics SDKs and no crash-reporting SDKs in StatusHD. No third party receives any information from this app.
