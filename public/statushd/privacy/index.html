Privacy Policy

StatusHD — com.statushd

Last updated: 26 September 2026 (contact setup, service records) — app version 3.0.8 (versionCode 54)

The app cannot send anything anywhere.

StatusHD does not request the INTERNET permission. That is not a policy it promises to follow — it is something the app is incapable of doing. It has no way to open a network connection, upload a file, or contact any server, including ours.

What it does is hand your chosen video to WhatsApp, and WhatsApp sends it, under its own permissions, when you choose to send it.

What the app collects

Nothing. There is no account, no sign-in, no password, no e-mail address and no phone number. No analytics or crash-reporting service is used. No advertising identifier is read. Nothing is sold, shared for advertising, or used to build a profile of you.

What the app does on your phone

  • Lists the videos already on your phone so you can pick one. That list never leaves the device.
  • Copies the video you pick into the app's own private cache, so it can be handed over under a name the service recognises. That copy is deleted the next time you send something, and when you uninstall the app.
  • Opens WhatsApp with that copy attached, as a document, addressed to the StatusHD service number. Sending it is your action, in WhatsApp.

The one-time setup: saving the service number

This belongs in this policy because it involves your phone's contacts and your phone number.

The first time you open StatusHD it asks you to save the service number (+91 90195 53799, as “StatusHD”) as a contact, and does not let you past that screen until you say you have.

This is not a sign-up and it creates no account. It exists because WhatsApp opens a chat directly only with a number it already knows: without it, sending a video lands on a list of your own contacts, and the service number is not in it.

  • The app opens your phone's own Contacts app with the name and number filled in; you press Save there. The app has no contacts permission and never reads your address book.
  • Saving the contact sends nothing to anyone. The service learns nothing from it.
  • If your phone has no Contacts app, the app offers instead to open WhatsApp with one message — “Hi, setting up StatusHD” — already written to the service number, through a wa.me link. That message is sent by you, from your own WhatsApp; the app cannot send it.
  • Once you send anything to the service number — a video, or that message — the person who operates the service can see that WhatsApp account's number and display name, exactly as any WhatsApp recipient can see any sender's.
  • The app itself still learns nothing and still sends nothing. It has no network.
  • You are asked once. Updating over an existing install does not ask again.

What happens after you send it

If you send it to the StatusHD service number, it arrives as a WhatsApp message on a personal computer operated by Sumathi Enterprises in Mangalore, India, which is signed in to WhatsApp like any other linked device. There, the video is:

  • downloaded from the conversation;
  • re-encoded so WhatsApp will accept it without ruining the quality — or copied byte for byte if it is already within limits;
  • sent back into the same conversation as a video;
  • deleted from that computer once it has been delivered. A prepared copy that could not be delivered is deleted automatically after 48 hours.
Two things to be aware of, and neither is softened.

That computer is a personal machine, not a data centre, and the person who operates it can open and watch any file on it while it is there.

And once a video has been sent through WhatsApp, a copy exists in that WhatsApp conversation and on Meta's servers. Removing it there is done inside WhatsApp, by deleting the message. Nothing on this page can do that for you.

Who else sees it

  • WhatsApp (Meta) carries the video in both directions, because delivering it through WhatsApp is the entire point. Their handling is governed by their own policy.

Telegram carries two things. Alerts to the service operator, which can include a conversation's name or phone number (for example, when a new number starts a free trial). And, for the few people the operator has named, videos they choose to send through Telegram instead of WhatsApp.

Cloudflare carries the operator's own control panel to the operator, behind a login. The panel shows the records above (names, numbers, file names). Videos do not pass through it. One address, /health, is public and says only whether the service is working — no names, numbers or file names.

Nobody else. No data broker, no advertising network.
