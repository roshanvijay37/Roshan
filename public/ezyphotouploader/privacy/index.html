Ezy Photo Uploader · Android

Privacy Policy

Ezy Photo Uploader moves photographs from your camera to your own Google Drive. Everything below describes an app that has no server behind it.

Effective 8 September 2026 Package com.roshanvijay.ezyphotouploader Developer Roshan Vijay

In short

  • There is no server. Your photographs travel from your camera to your phone to your Google Drive, and nowhere else.
  • The developer cannot see your photographs, your Drive, or your account.
  • No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no tracking, no third parties.
  • Nothing is sold, shared or transmitted to anyone.

01 Who this policy is from

Ezy Photo Uploader is published by Roshan Vijay, an individual developer, and this policy covers the Android application com.roshanvijay.ezyphotouploader and nothing else.

02 The app has no backend

This is the fact that determines everything else. The app runs an FTP receiver on your phone, holds a queue on your phone, and uploads directly from your phone to Google Drive using the Google Drive API.

There is no server operated by the developer — no account system, no database, no logging service, no cloud storage. Your photographs are never sent to the developer, because there is nowhere for them to be sent.

03 What the app stores on your phone

All of the following lives in the private storage area of the app, readable only by this app, and is removed entirely when you uninstall it:

  • Photographs received from your camera, held until they have uploaded and you delete the event.
  • An upload queue — filenames, file sizes, a content checksum used to detect duplicates, upload status and error messages. This is a database on disk so that a queue survives the app being closed or the phone restarting.
  • Event names that you type, and the Drive folder id the app created for each one.
  • Settings — the FTP port and password, and your upload preferences.
  • A diagnostic log of connection and upload events, kept on the device to help you troubleshoot. Passwords are masked before they reach it. It is never uploaded anywhere.

04 Your Google account and your Drive

You sign in with your own Google account so that the app can upload to your own Drive. Sign-in is handled by Google Play services; the app receives short-lived access tokens from Google and stores no password and no long-lived token of its own.

The app requests exactly one permission, the narrowest one that can write to Drive:

  • drive.file — access limited to files and folders this app itself created. The app cannot read, list, modify or delete anything else in your Drive. Your existing documents and photographs are invisible to it.

Your email address is shown in the app so you can confirm which account is in use. It is read from Google Play services on demand and is not transmitted anywhere by the app.

Data you upload to Google Drive is then governed by the Google Privacy Policy and by your own Drive settings.

05 Sharing a folder by QR code

The app can generate a QR code linking to the Drive folder for an event. This is off unless you turn it on, per event.

Turning it on asks Google to make that one folder viewable by anyone who has the link. That is a deliberate act of publication: anyone you give the code to, and anyone they pass it to, can view the photographs in that folder. Only the folder you chose is affected. You can revoke it at any time from the app, or from Google Drive directly.

06 What the app does not do

Stated plainly, because absence is hard to prove from the outside:

  • No analytics or usage tracking of any kind is built into the app.
  • No crash-reporting service. No advertising, and no advertising identifier is read.
  • No location is requested or recorded. The app does not hold the location permission.
  • No contacts, calendar, microphone, call or SMS data is accessed.
  • No data is sold, rented, shared or disclosed to any third party.
  • No account is created with the developer, because there is no such account to create.

08 Security, honestly stated

The link between your camera and your phone uses plain FTP, not FTPS, because the camera hardware this app is built for requires a certificate installed on the camera to do otherwise. It is protected by a username and password you control, and is intended for the hotspot of your own phone, or a network you trust.

On a network you do not control, someone on that same network could in principle observe that transfer. Use the phone hotspot, which is the recommended setup and the one the documentation describes.

The upload from your phone to Google Drive is always encrypted over HTTPS.

09 Keeping and deleting your data

Photographs stay on the phone until you delete the event or uninstall the app. Uninstalling removes every file, the queue database, the settings and the log — Android deletes the private storage of the app along with it.

Photographs already uploaded to Google Drive belong to your Drive and are unaffected by uninstalling; delete them there if you want them gone.

You can withdraw access to your Drive at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is no data for the developer to delete, export or correct on request.

10 Children

This app is a tool for photographers and is not directed at children. It does not knowingly collect anything from anyone, of any age.
